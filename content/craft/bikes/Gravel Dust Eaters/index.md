## The sunset came first

- The good part was the sunset: high ground, red dirt going gold, nobody around.
- Then we went the wrong way, and the light started to matter.
- Nobody in this group rides in the dark, so we rushed the last stretch.
- That's when the track turned out to be someone's driveway.
- Dogs. An army of them, and one very large white one who was not a good boy.
- Nothing happened. We got out. But that's the shape of the ride: reward first, then the bill.

## This is not a gravel bike

- These are titanium road frames. On paper the clearance stops well short of a gravel bike.
- So the wheels came down: 700c with 28c slicks out, 650B aluminium in — Ritchey, not carbon.
- That's the whole trick. Smaller wheel, same frame, room for a 38 mm GravelKing semi-slick.
- Measured, they blow up to 40 mm. Deliberate, not accidental.
- Why it matters: more air, more give, proper puncture protection against the gravelly bits — without buying a second bike.
- Frame it as a constraint, not a compromise: not "go anywhere", but "don't turn around when the surface changes".
- Singapore is fifty kilometres across and every metre is tarmac — gravel isn't something you can practise at home.

The plan was never to buy a gravel bike. The plan was to make the road bikes survive the roads the trip actually had.

## Pressure does most of the work

- Pressures are where the 650B swap pays off: 40–50 psi, not road pressures.
- At road pressure a narrow tyre on gravel skitters — the bike goes where the stones point it, and you spend the descent braking.
- Softer and the tyre starts to conform: it finds the shape of the surface instead of bouncing off it. Same frame, same rider, different bike.
- The catch is pinch flats — which is part of why the semi-slick with proper protection matters here.

## What it costs to keep it

- Dust in the chain, on the brake tracks, inside the bag straps by the second day.
- Cleaning is a bitch. Say it plainly — it's the honest counterweight to the romance.
- Titanium helps: it takes dings, doesn't rust or corrode, and doesn't ask for much beyond a clean.
- Mud is the real limit — fine by dry gravel, but clay would pack the stays quickly.

## Why I keep going off-road

- Road riding can be passive. Long straight, zone out, arrive.
- Gravel isn't, at any speed: you're picking a line, correcting, reading the surface, feeling for the skid.
- Not a gravel racer. The attention is the point, not the speed.
- Something romantic about white dust on a titanium frame — a bike that's clearly been used.
- The freedom of it: get on, look after the gear, and it takes you somewhere.
- And the rhyme: the surface lies. The dust looks dead. The map says the road is sealed.

## Was it worth it

Yes — but not as a second bike. The honest version is that these frames were already the right answer: titanium, enough clearance once the wheels came down to 650B, and a geometry that does not punish you for being off tarmac for an hour. Gravel mode was a wheel change, a tyre change and a pressure change — not a build.
