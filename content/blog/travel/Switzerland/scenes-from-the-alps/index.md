There's a particular quiet that settles in when the road tilts upward and the last town disappears behind you. No engine noise. No notifications. Just the rhythm of breath and the slow rotation of cranks.

This wasn't a race. It was three days of moving through the Swiss Alps the only way that makes sense to me — slowly enough to feel every contour of the climb.

{{< /content-block >}}

The summit doesn't reward speed. It rewards persistence. One pedal stroke at a time.

## Susten — The Long Game

Day one starts with the longest climb of the trip. Seventeen kilometres from Wassen to the Sustenpass summit, averaging 7.2%. The road rises through forest, then opens into alpine meadow, then finally delivers you to the moonscape above treeline.

The descent to Meiringen is 30 kilometres of pure gravity — the kind of payoff that makes you forget every aching moment of the climb.

## Grimsel — The Grind

Day two is the purest climbing day of the three. No descending. No breaks. Thirty-five kilometres, 1,694 metres of elevation — all upward.

The road follows the Aare river through a landscape of granite and grey-green water. The gradient sits at 5-7% for hour after hour. At the top, Hotel Grimsel Passhöhe sits at the edge of Totensee, overlooking the lake.

Nothing in life teaches you about sustained effort quite like thirty-five kilometres of continuous climbing.

## Furka — The Icon

Day three. Wake at the top of Grimsel, drop down to Gletsch, then take on the Furka — the pass from _Goldfinger_, the one with the switchbacks you've seen in photographs for years.

Ten kilometres, 910 metres, 6.5% average. The actual gradient matters less than the fact that each zig-zag delivers a view more dramatic than the last. The Rhône glacier gleams to your left. The valley floor spins far below.

And then the summit. And then twenty kilometres of descending back into Andermatt, where your car and luggage are waiting, and the whole thing is done.

## The Numbers

**127.2 km** total distance. **3,766 m** total climbing. Three days, three summits, one loop.

More importantly: three mornings of waking up in a new valley, three evenings of beer at altitude, and three chances to feel very, very small in the middle of the Alps.

That's the whole point, really.
